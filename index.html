Homeowners’ association FAQs

What is a homeowners’ association?

Automatic and mandatory homeowners’ associations are part of an overall concept of residential property ownership. Purchase of a home or lot brings membership in the association, which provides the structure for operating and managing the community. Membership includes mandatory obligations, financial responsibilities, and association rules.

What is the overall purpose of the HOA?

The community association gives continuity to the community, preserves its architectural integrity, maintains common property, and promotes the community concept while protecting property values.

What is the purpose of the governing documents?

A community association’s governing documents provide the legal structure for the association and explain how it operates. They:

  • Define the rights and obligations of the association and its owners.
  • Create a binding relationship between each owner and the association.
  • Establish how the association governs and funds its operations.
  • Set rules and standards to protect owners and the community.
  • Help protect property values and promote harmonious living.
What do my dues cover?

Community association dues, also called assessments, may be paid monthly, quarterly, or annually. Depending on the association, they may cover landscaping and upkeep of common areas, repairs and maintenance of amenities, social activities, and insurance.

What doesn’t the HOA do?

HOA management serves residents within the scope of the association’s responsibilities. It generally does not mediate private disputes between neighbors or handle domestic disputes, replace law enforcement, provide police protection, or control city or county services.

What are CC&Rs?

Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions are rules that help maintain design consistency and the character of a community. Review your community’s CC&Rs and obtain any required approval before making exterior changes or improvements to your home or lot. Builders typically handle approvals for initial construction.

What is the role of the board of directors?

The board manages the association under its governing documents and applicable law. Its responsibilities can include setting policies, enforcing the governing documents, maintaining common property, managing finances, purchasing insurance, hiring service providers, overseeing committees, and holding annual and board meetings.
